# Paper List

A running list of notable papers, with brief summaries.

## Environmental / Economic Science

- **Burke, M., Zahid, M., Diffenbaugh, N. S., & Hsiang, S.** (2026). *Quantifying climate loss and damage consistent with a social cost of carbon*. **Nature**, 651, 959–966. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10272-6

  **Summary:** This study builds the first quantitative framework linking specific past and future CO2 emissions to monetized, location-specific climate damages, tying the accounting directly to the social cost of carbon. Using empirical estimates of the non-linear relationship between temperature and aggregate economic output, the authors find that the bulk of the economic damage from any given tonne of CO2 is still to come: a tonne emitted in 1990 caused about $180 in discounted global damages by 2020, but is projected to cause an additional $1,840 through 2100 — roughly ten times more. This means historical "loss and damage" accounting based only on harm already incurred vastly understates the true liability of past emissions. The findings matter for international climate finance and loss-and-damage negotiations, since they suggest far larger future costs are already locked in from emissions released decades ago, with direct implications for how climate liability and reparations should be calculated going forward.

- **Chen, X., Guo, X., Yang, X., Li, J., & McElroy, M. B.** (2026). *Hydrogen derivatives enable cost-effective carbon neutrality in China*. **Nature** (Accelerated Article Preview, published online 5 November 2026). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11351-4

  **Summary:** This study asks whether clean hydrogen and the fuels made from it (ammonia, methanol, synthetic methane, dimethyl ether and Fischer–Tropsch fuels) can make China's move to carbon neutrality cheaper than relying on renewables and electrification alone. The authors built a whole-energy-system model with high-resolution renewable siting, hourly power operations across 29 provincial regions, more than 46,000 rail, road, marine and pipeline routes for moving hydrogen derivatives, and process-level fuel choices in every end-use sector. In the abstract, they report that adding hydrogen derivatives cuts the total transition cost by about US$10.7 trillion, gives a negative average abatement cost of roughly −$6.15 per tonne of CO2, and removes oil import spending. They say the import savings equal more than half of China's 2023 trade surplus. Because China is the world's largest emitter, the results suggest that targeted hydrogen-derivative supply chains could lower the cost of decarbonising hard-to-electrify sectors and support energy security, though the headline savings depend on the scenario and cost assumptions.
