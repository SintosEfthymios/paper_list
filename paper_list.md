# Paper List

A running list of notable papers, with brief summaries.

## Environmental / Economic Science

- **Burke, M., Zahid, M., Diffenbaugh, N. S., & Hsiang, S.** (2026). *Quantifying climate loss and damage consistent with a social cost of carbon*. **Nature**, 651, 959–966. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10272-6

  **Summary:** This study builds the first quantitative framework linking specific past and future CO2 emissions to monetized, location-specific climate damages, tying the accounting directly to the social cost of carbon. Using empirical estimates of the non-linear relationship between temperature and aggregate economic output, the authors find that the bulk of the economic damage from any given tonne of CO2 is still to come: a tonne emitted in 1990 caused about $180 in discounted global damages by 2020, but is projected to cause an additional $1,840 through 2100 — roughly ten times more. This means historical "loss and damage" accounting based only on harm already incurred vastly understates the true liability of past emissions. The findings matter for international climate finance and loss-and-damage negotiations, since they suggest far larger future costs are already locked in from emissions released decades ago, with direct implications for how climate liability and reparations should be calculated going forward.

- **Berryman, A. K., Bücker, J., Senra de Moura, F., Barbrook-Johnson, P., Hanusch, M., Mealy, P., Farmer, J. D., & del Rio-Chanona, R. M.** (2026). *Skill and spatial mismatches for sustainable development in Brazil*. **Nature Sustainability**. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-026-01939-z

  **Summary:** This study looks at how Brazil's choice of development path, in particular one that cuts deforestation, affects workers. It does so by pairing a network of how easily workers can move between occupations and regions with an agent-based labour-market simulation. The authors compare an agriculture-led growth scenario with a green-manufacturing scenario. They find that the pathways that cut deforestation and emissions the most generally lower unemployment for high-skill, high-wage workers. The same pathways raise unemployment for low-skill workers, especially in agriculture, because those workers find it hardest to change jobs or move. Agriculture-led growth causes more labour reallocation and higher overall unemployment than manufacturing-led growth, and policies that make it easier for workers to change occupations or regions substantially reduce the harm. The work matters because it shows that the social cost of conservation-friendly growth depends heavily on labour-market frictions. Targeted support for the least mobile workers can make forest protection compatible with a just transition.
